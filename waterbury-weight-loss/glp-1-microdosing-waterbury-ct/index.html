    How GLP-1 Microdosing Works in Waterbury

    Microdosing is a dosing strategy, not a separate drug. It means taking semaglutide or tirzepatide at doses lower than the standard labeled schedule, or increasing the dose more slowly, while the medicine still works on the same gut hormone receptors. Some people notice quieter food noise. Low doses usually require compounded medicine, which lacks FDA approval.

    Who GLP-1 Microdosing Is For

    Three groups ask about it most. People who could not tolerate standard GLP-1 doses because of nausea, people who want a slower start and people maintaining weight loss after a full course are the usual candidates. It is not a shortcut. Claims that microdosing treats inflammation or aging are not backed by strong evidence.

    Low-Dose Schedule in Waterbury

    There is no single standard schedule. A common approach begins at a fraction of the labeled starting dose, like part of 0.25 mg semaglutide or 2.5 mg tirzepatide, and moves up in smaller increments. Some split the weekly amount. Any change should go through your provider first.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Early weeks vary a lot between people. Some notice less food noise and earlier fullness within the first two weeks, while others notice almost nothing at a very low dose and need a small increase to feel an effect. Nausea is usually milder. Expect modest, gradual change.

    Side Effects in Waterbury

    Lower doses reduce side effects but do not erase them. Nausea, constipation, diarrhea, reflux and reduced appetite are still the most common, especially in the days after any dose increase, even a small one. Serious risks still apply. GLP-1 risks like pancreatitis and gallstones, and the rodent thyroid tumor warning, do not go away at low doses.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Screening is the same as for full doses. The review covers BMI, weight history, diagnoses, medications and earlier GLP-1 use, with questions about the dose you reached and what made you stop. MEN 2 is a hard stop. Pregnancy, pancreatitis, gastroparesis and eating disorders all need careful review.

    Microdosing Compared With Standard GLP-1 Treatment in Waterbury

    Standard dosing has the trial data. The STEP and SURMOUNT trials tested full doses and reported average losses near 15 to 20 percent, and nothing comparable exists for low-dose plans. Expect smaller average results. Maintenance is a common use case.
